Run `scripts/audit_duplex_ifc.py data/external/duplex/Duplex_A_20110907.ifc --output runs/duplex-audit` then `scripts/run_controlled_benchmark.py --building-audit runs/duplex-audit --output runs/duplex-controlled`. The audit enforces the expected source hash, inventories global/STEP IDs and space boundaries, triangulates in world coordinates and unions XY triangles for each space. It retains exterior/hole rings and errors; no bounding-box replacement of room polygons is used. Storey elevations come from the audited IFC (0 and 3.10000000000038 metres). Mesh projection is an explicit 2D adaptation, not recovery of an authored architectural plan.

Two subsets use the A101/A201 and B101/B201 foyer/hallway pairs. They are eight controlled variants of **one** source building, not independent buildings. All selected IFC room IDs and extracted projected exterior coordinates remain unchanged. The selected projections have no holes and are orthogonal under the shared tolerance. Floor bounding rectangles are experimental enclosing domains, not IFC slab outlines. Within each one-room floor subset A is empty before and after. No adjacent spaces are merged or deleted to improve parameters: the subset and its restricted obligations are explicit. Other rooms are outside the evaluated case; this does not establish access to all destinations in the building.

Both existing stairs are accounted for as conservative XY obstacle rectangles computed from all represented decomposition parts, including flights, members and railings. Those envelopes are carried on both experimental floors. The stairs remain physically present and cannot admit a hypothetical lift; their rectangular envelopes can reject more space than exact projected solids. Stair permissions are absent from the experimental profile graphs because both hypothetical profiles are incapable of stair use. This is a controlled graph assumption, not an extracted accessibility judgment. No independent-background correction extension is enabled. Other IFC walls/doors are not transformed into new traversable relations; the experiment uses isolated room-subset graphs. Fixtures, headroom, slabs and equipment operation remain outside the named kernel.

## Experimental assumptions (not source facts)

Profiles experimental0/1 have square widths and clear-patch requirements 0.4/0.5 metres, stair capability false and ramp slope 0.1 (unused). These are experimental values, not code thresholds. Each subset receives two hypothetical 0.7-by-0.7 metre rectangular lift reservations, landings and fixed envelopes; vertical rise equals the IFC storey separation. The data do not establish pre-existing shaft openings or constructibility. All candidate IDs are clearly prefixed in evidence/ownership records as hypothetical. No claimed building restoration reference exists.

A reservations: [6.5,7.2] x [-10.8,-10.1] and [6.5,7.2] x [-9.8,-9.1]. B reservations: [1.6,2.3] x [-9.3,-8.6] and [1.6,2.3] x [-8.3,-7.6]. Geometric containment/clearance/collision are recomputed. Reservations are author assumptions, not inferred from overlapping spaces.

A buys both lifts for cost 3 abstract units, with one profile permission per lift. B/C buy the corresponding lift for cost 2 each. D buys the first lift with both permissions for cost 4. All physical capability sets support both hypothetical profiles. Four variants per apartment: base bundle catalogue; bundle price 5 with D removed (separate optimum B+C); only B (profile1 impossible); only D (single optimum). These are economic/permission controls, not real price data or metadata restoration.
