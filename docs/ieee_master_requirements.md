# IEEE master-paper requirements and evidence assessment

Checked 14 October 2026 using primary IEEE guidance; no target conference has been selected. This is an adaptable detailed master manuscript, not certification of acceptance or venue-specific compliance.

## What IEEE expects

The conference Author Center lists scope, novelty, validity, data, clarity, compliance and advancement as review criteria: https://conferences.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/understand-peer-review/ . Its structure guide asks for an informative title, a self-contained single-paragraph abstract (up to 250 words, no equations/references), motivation and exact question, reproducible methods, interpreted results and limitations, and directly relevant references: https://conferences.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/write-your-paper/structure-your-paper/ . The tools page recommends official templates and code/data sharing: https://conferences.ieeeauthorcenter.ieee.org/write-your-paper/authoring-tools-and-templates/ . The general publishing page requires removal of template guidance text: https://events.ieee.org/planning-basics/ieee-conference-publications/publishing-information-for-ieee-conference-authors/ . None gives a universal minimum number of figures, datasets or references, nor a universal page limit or acceptance guarantee.

The supplied ZIP is the June 2024 IEEE conference template using IEEEtran V1.8b. Its examples are reference material, not instructions from the user: sample authors/funding/references/results are discarded. The unchanged class is reusable under its included LPPL 1.3 notice; no margins/fonts/column widths are altered. Figures get captions below, tables captions above, and numbered cross-references. The master draft uses anonymous author metadata because author details were not supplied. This is not a claim that every conference is double-anonymous; actual authorship/anonymity rules must be applied later.

IEEE policy requires disclosure of generated text, figures or code, identifying the system, affected sections and extent: https://open.ieee.org/author-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-ai-generated-text/ . The master includes an accurate Codex/OpenAI disclosure; it does not claim human proof approval occurred. Authors remain responsible for final verification. No paper or data is uploaded to an external checker or submitted here.

## What does not establish acceptance

My interpretation of the criteria: IEEE-looking formatting, test counts, decorative diagrams, a passing demo, a larger reference list, or an unqualified claim of novelty cannot replace a substantive contribution and valid evaluation. Unfair baselines, treating rejected/limited scope as infeasibility, presenting hypothetical reservations as actual buildings, or assigning a new objective without labeling it undermine validity/data interpretation. Conference-specific scope, length, anonymity and ethics rules remain unknown until a venue is chosen.

## Supported contribution and remaining risks

The positive working contribution is the exact ownership/full-action transfer into a rooted floor-interface state, with explicit per-cell owner multiplicity and cost/count/ID semantics, plus reproducible verification/evaluation. Weighted labels and representative partition bases are prior work. The sharper exponent than one constructed generic upper bound does not prove priority or universal superiority. A matching generic/domain-based method remains possible; algorithmic originality is the main unresolved scientific risk. Do not claim that new semantic controls establish literature novelty.

The new semantic-control protocol uses three existing authored inputs and four deliberate simplifications: graph-only occupancy omission (two cases), forbidding same-owner reuse, and charging an original action once per effect. It reuses saved small-oracle ledgers/certificates, exhaustively evaluates each small control and replays its selected set in the full kernel. This answers why full ownership/geometry/atomic costs matter; these controls are not valid competing repair algorithms or runtime baselines. Every control gets input/source/seed/environment/status and source-certificate hashes. Only targeted tests/new controls run; the 620-test release checkpoint is not relabeled as 621 tests run together.

Existing timing evidence has only three samples and local workstation load. Charts show all samples and medians, with no significance/speedup claim. Current building evidence has eight variants of one licensed source with explicit hypothetical assumptions. It cannot support claims of multiple independent buildings, actual cost savings or constructibility. The detailed master keeps these limits and supplies an appendix/venue adaptation checklist, while preserving the fixed project direction.
